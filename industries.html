By industry

Pop-up shops built for
your category.

A beauty pop-up isn’t a fashion pop-up isn’t a food activation. Here’s what each industry needs from a New York pop-up — and how we produce it.

Every category sells differently, so every pop-up gets built differently. Below is how we approach the industries we produce for most in New York. The through-line is the same: we source the space, run the build, staff the run, and hand you a store ready to open.

Beauty & fragrance

Beauty pop-ups live on touch and light. Testers, sampling stations, clean mirrors, and a hygienic try-on flow decide whether people linger. We build sensory moments — scent walls, gifting stations, seated consultations — and get the lighting right so product and skin read true. Best neighborhoods: SoHo for reach, Nolita for intimacy.

Fashion & streetwear

Fashion and streetwear pop-ups are about merchandising and drop-day energy. Fitting rooms, lookbook walls, and clean sightlines to the hero product; on launch days, queue management and security that keep the line an asset, not a liability. We match the label to the block — SoHo and Nolita for established fashion, Williamsburg for emerging and DTC.

Footwear & sneakers

Sneaker launches are events. Display walls that make the silhouette the hero, raffle and reservation flow, and a build that survives a crowd. We produce the drop from storefront to strike, including the staffing and security a hyped release needs.

Food & beverage

F&B pop-ups carry the most operational weight: health permits, hand-wash and prep requirements, ventilation, and queue flow. We handle the licensing and the build so a tasting bar, café takeover, or product launch runs clean and to code.

Jewelry & accessories

High-consideration product wants a considered, secure setting. Casework, controlled lighting, seated selling, and security appropriate to the price point. Tribeca and quieter SoHo blocks suit the pace.

Tech & DTC

For online-first and tech brands, the pop-up is a first physical home and a demo space. Interactive stations, clear product storytelling, and data capture that connects the room back to the site. Williamsburg and Flatiron are natural fits for a first store.
