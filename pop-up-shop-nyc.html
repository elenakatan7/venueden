The guide

Opening a pop-up shop
in New York City.

What a pop-up space really costs, how fast you can launch, and where it should open. From the team that does this for a living.

What is a pop-up shop?

A short-term retail store. Days, weeks, or a few months, used to launch a product, test a neighborhood, run a seasonal moment, or give an online brand its first physical home. In New York that can mean a capsule drop in a SoHo storefront or a full build with fabrication, custom lighting, and staffed opening nights.

How much does a pop-up shop cost in NYC?

Most pop-up projects start around $12K. Five things move the number:

  • The space. Neighborhood, size, and length of run drive rent more than anything else.
  • The build-out. Paint and merchandising is one budget; custom millwork and fabrication is another.
  • The timeline. Compressed launches cost more in expedited trades and freight.
  • Staffing. Floor staff, security, and cleaning scale with hours and foot traffic.
  • Production needs. Signage, furniture rental, AV, photography, event nights.

Every project is quoted individually. Tell us what you’re planning and you’ll have a real number within one business day.

How fast can a pop-up launch?

Typically four to eight weeks from brief to open: one to three weeks to source and sign the space, then two to five weeks of design, build, and merchandising. Tighter is doable when the brief is clear.

Where should a pop-up open in NYC?

The neighborhood is half of why a pop-up works. The short version:

  • SoHo. Flagship energy, global foot traffic. Where established brands make statements.
  • Nolita. Intimate blocks, loyal locals. Good for emerging labels and jewel-box formats.
  • Tribeca. Quiet luxury at a collector’s pace. Showrooms and high-consideration product.
  • Williamsburg. Young, early, first-to-know. The right first store for DTC brands.
  • Lower East Side & beyond. Culture-led launches, galleries, music moments.

We scout storefronts anywhere in New York, not from a fixed portfolio, and match the brand to the block: foot traffic, neighbors, how the street feels at 6pm. See a fuller neighborhood-by-neighborhood guide to renting a pop-up space.

What does it take to produce a pop-up?

More than most brands expect. A short-term lease with insurance and permits. Design, construction, fabrication. Lighting, fixtures, signage. Freight, receiving, merchandising. Staff, security, cleaning. Then a proper strike that returns the keys broom-clean. See everything we handle. One team, one point of contact.
